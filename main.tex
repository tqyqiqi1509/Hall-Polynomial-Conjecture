\begin{abstract}
We prove that every representation-finite algebra over a finite field has Hall polynomials with integer coefficients. The polynomials count submodules with fixed submodule, quotient and middle-module isomorphism types over every conservative finite extension.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Let $k=\FF_q$ and let $A$ be a finite-dimensional $k$-algebra of finite representation type. For a finite-dimensional right $A$-module $M$ and a finite extension $E/k$, write $M_E=M\otimes_kE$. For such modules $X,Y,L$, define
\[
F^L_{X,Y}(E)=\#\{U\subseteq L_E:U\simeq Y_E,\ L_E/U\simeq X_E\}.
\]
The first lower index is the quotient and the second is the submodule. A finite extension is \emph{conservative for $A$} if every indecomposable $A$-module remains indecomposable after scalar extension. We say that $A$ has Hall polynomials if, for every $X,Y,L$, there is a polynomial $\varphi^L_{X,Y}\in\ZZ[T]$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hall}
 F^L_{X,Y}(E)=\varphi^L_{X,Y}(|E|)
 \qquad\text{for every conservative }E/k.
\end{equation}
Hall polynomials for representation-finite hereditary algebras were established by Ringel \cite{Ringel}. Hall polynomials for nilpotent cyclic-quiver representations provide another basic counting input; see \cite[Theorem 16]{Hubery}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Every representation-finite algebra over a finite field has Hall polynomials.
\end{theorem}

The proof first treats geometrically standard algebras. A representation-finite algebra over an algebraically closed field is \emph{mesh-standard} if its category of indecomposable modules is equivalent, with its object labels, to the mesh category of its Auslander--Reiten quiver. We call $A$ \emph{geometrically standard} if $A_{\bar k}$ is mesh-standard, where $\bar k$ is an algebraic closure of $k$. The mesh relations come from almost split sequences; their finite-field modulation is described in Section~\ref{sec:structure}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:standard}
Every geometrically standard representation-finite algebra over a finite field has Hall polynomials.
\end{theorem}

For Theorem~\ref{thm:standard}, the contravariant defect of a short exact sequence determines its minimal endpoints and middle term through its dimension vector, top and socle. An orbit formula expresses Hall numbers through the masses of these defects. We compute the masses on the stable Auslander algebra by completing finite translation intervals, decomposing local data into strings and bands, and evaluating the quadratic character sums. Total masses on periodic Dynkin components determine the remaining band contribution.

The standard-form classification \cite{Bongartz13} reduces the additional characteristic-two cases to penny configurations. Their radical-to-socle-quotient maps differ by a root shear. For each graph map into a quotient module, we extend that shear modulo maps through projectives; pullback by a target automorphism then permutes extension classes while preserving the required middle-module labels. At isolated roots this gives all Hall numbers of the standard form. The three possible root attachments have two exceptional indecomposables; matrix-rank counts and the recursions in Section~\ref{sec:attachments} determine their remaining Hall coefficients. Section~\ref{sec:global} completes the proof over the original finite field by node splitting and central restriction of scalars.
